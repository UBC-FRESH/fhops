Gavin Lake — Beaver Valley MDWR no-clearcut / group-selection screen

Status: constraint-translation screen, not an approved plan or compliance determination.

Constraint

The 2007 AFRF Management and Working Plan #3 makes mule-deer winter range the primary objective in the Beaver Valley working circle:

  • Beaver Valley MDWR: 2,799.31 ha, 1,443 blocks;
  • no clearcut in the MDWR;
  • group selection with a 40-year re-entry;
  • Table 30 pass intensity by habitat class: High 33%, Moderate 25%, Low 20%;
  • maximum group size 0.4 ha on warm aspects and 0.7 ha on other aspects.

The current public Gavin policy screen (gavin-policy) does not carry the constraint: it schedules 2,759 ha of MDWR and all 8,980 events are clearcut. This screen replaces clearcut with group selection on MDWR tracks and reports the production consequence.

Habitat-class assumption

The available legacy spatial layer has WORKING_CI = MDWR, but it does not carry the official Appendix 9 High/Moderate/Low habitat-class map. This screen uses a documented BEC proxy:

Proxy class BEC Area Blocks Pass intensity
High ICHmk3 14.27 ha 19 33%
Moderate ICHmk3/SBSdw 213.06 ha 136 25%
Low SBSdw1 2,571.98 ha 1,288 20%

The proxy is a screening assumption, not a legal habitat-class delineation.

Method

  • Clone the legacy tracks used by MDWR fragments.
  • Remove clearcut from the MDWR clones.
  • Add Table 30 group-selection treatments at the BEC-proxy pass intensity.
  • First entry at age 80; 40-year re-entry after that.
  • Scale product yield, treated area and carbon removal by the pass fraction. The block remains a full-area partial-cut volume screen rather than a spatial group layout.
  • Run the translated 300-year schedule against the existing public Gavin policy target set (174,000 m³ per period, 600,000 m³ period-30 stock floor, ±4% even-flow).

The MDWR-constrained group-selection screen reduces total production by about 31% against the timber-only upper bound and does not meet the existing policy floor. The deferral floor is lower again in timber but leaves much more standing stock.

The target status is reported against the existing 174k policy target set. It is a trade-off result, not a failure hidden by a redefined target.

Official habitat-map acquisition

The public BC long-term habitat objective map for Beaver Valley North (Prouton Lakes) has now been acquired and digitized. It gives a fine-scale High/Moderate/Low split for the MDWR that is very different from the BEC proxy:

Class BEC proxy Official fine map
High 14.27 ha 682.3 ha
Moderate 213.06 ha 1,115.4 ha
Low 2,571.98 ha 898.4 ha

The official polygons cover 84.6% of the MDWR block area. The adjusted Appendix 9 compartment map still has to be reconciled with these fine polygons, and the 2007 legal order and current UWR boundary still need review. The results in this brief therefore still use the BEC proxy; the model has not been rebuilt with the official classes.

A re-entry audit also found that the current MDWR clone resets the fragment age on every pass, while the pass product curves start at age 79. In a block test, the first pass produced volume and every later 40-year pass produced zero. The public 3.735M m³ result is therefore a first-entry / full-area partial-cut screen, not a validated 40-year re-entry result. The official-class matrix is built but not published.

Limits

  • The official Appendix 9 compartment map has not been reconciled with the fine-scale BC long-term objective polygons; the BEC proxy must be replaced before a compliance claim.
  • The block-level partial-cut curve is a volume screen. It does not lay out spatial groups, enforce the 0.4/0.7 ha group-size cap, or simulate within-block cohort structure.
  • Preparatory thinning at least ten years before group selection is not modelled.
  • No public AFRF private finance ledger is used or fabricated.
  • The 2007 legal order and current UWR boundary still need review.

Decision boundary

This screen demonstrates the size and direction of the MDWR trade-off. It does not approve an AAC, a group-selection layout, or a legal habitat-class assignment. The next technical step is to obtain the official Appendix 9 habitat-class map and replace the BEC proxy before using the result in a management decision.
