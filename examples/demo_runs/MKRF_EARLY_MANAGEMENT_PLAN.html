1. Executive summary

MKRF is the largest and most mature model instance in the current WS4/FHOPS stack:

  • 1,763 blocks;
  • 4,810.85 ha;
  • a 100-year planning horizon in ten 10-year periods;
  • three full-estate scenarios (base-44, low-44, high-44);
  • public net-revenue and harvest-cost accounts;
  • P00/P01 operational handoffs;
  • actual 30-day FHOPS Phase I/II haul samples and a rolling full-year Phase I aggregate.

Retention-value caveat

The low/base/high variants are area-retention model rebuilds. WS4 moves a fraction of eligible fragment area to an unmanaged state; it does not select species, DBH, or the largest Douglas-fir stems. The current numbers therefore carry an average area slice, not the high-graded residual cohort described in the field.

A retention-value audit is in MKRF_RETENTION_AUDIT.md. The audit uses the pinned MKRF 684 tree list to show that the largest 5% of stems contain about 33% of gross value.

I also rebuilt the no-retention matrix and repaired its product curve table against the canonical matrix. The no-retention baseline is now:

  • gross log value: CAD 716.7M
  • production: 3.38M m³
  • net after all cost: CAD 403.7M
  • gross NPV 4%: CAD 182.6M
  • net NPV 4%: CAD 100.9M

I then built residual-product matrices and re-ran the same target search for the three residual cases. The product ledgers carry the largest-DF value/volume reduction, but that product screen still harvests the full area.

I also built a state-aware value-equivalent residual-area variant. Each no-retention block is split into a harvested track and a residual track; the residual track carries copied feature curves, ages through the horizon and is not re-entered in this screen. The harvested net/cost curves include a fixed-cost correction, so the state totals reconcile with the product-ledger screen. Residual areas are 1,567 ha (low), 2,055 ha (base) and 2,652 ha (high). The low state run resolves one additional feature-yield target (32/4/1 instead of 31/5/1); base and high are unchanged.

2. Management context

MKRF is a separate UBC Research Forest instance, not an AFRF block. It is the flagship test of the WS4 runtime because it has:

  • a large block count and a real spatial topology;
  • mature WS4 probability/account surfaces;
  • public cost and price bases;
  • an operational FHOPS handoff;
  • a scenario set that can be compared on production, targets and NPV.

The management-plan structure should preserve the research forest’s multiple mandates: research and teaching, timber production, ecological values, recreation, and transparent public reporting.

The current model does not yet carry every non-timber constraint that a final MKRF plan will need. The scenario set is therefore a timber-and-finance screening surface, not a complete land-use plan.

4. Modelling approach

The MKRF evidence chain is:

FEMIC rebuild / public inventory
        ↓
WS4 compiled matrix (tracks, curves, products, accounts)
        ↓
targets, patch accounts, topology and route configuration
        ↓
WS4 scenario search and simulation
        ↓
FHOPS tactical / operational / hauling handoffs
        ↓
public KPI and finance screens

The ws4/runtime/matrix_silv dataset is the main runtime surface. The scenarios are not just alternate maps: they carry different schedules and target responses, and the public console exposes their production, accounts, targets, patch metrics, and operational handoffs.

4.1 Scenario construction

  • Ten 10-year periods, 100-year horizon.
  • base-44, low-44, and high-44 from the WS4 scenario family.
  • Public net-revenue and harvest-cost accounts.
  • Patch-size metrics and opening-size policy included in reports.
  • P00/P01 operational handoffs with zero remaining work.
  • Actual 30-day Phase I/II hauling samples and a rolling full-year Phase I aggregate.

The scenario comparison is intentionally a family, not a single optimum. A low, base and high case shows the decision space and the sensitivity of the target response.

6. Scenario plan for review

The recommended review set is:

  1. low-44 — primary public screen, best target performance.
  2. base-44 — central comparison against the original scenario family.
  3. high-44 — stress case for reduced production and lower NPV.
  4. low P00/P01 operations — first two decades of implementation.
  5. 30-day and rolling full-year haul — fleet and delivery screening.
  6. A future ecological constraint case — not yet built; should add old forest, riparian, visual and wildlife constraints before the plan is treated as complete.

The choice rule should not be “highest NPV.” It should be:

  • does the scenario meet the planning targets or explain the misses?
  • does the operational screen close?
  • is the opening/patch pattern acceptable?
  • are the non-timber values represented?
  • is the financial result a public screen or an audited budget?

7. What the numbers can and cannot support

What is strong:

  • block geometry and estate coverage;
  • WS4 runtime and scenario reproducibility;
  • production, account, target and patch reporting;
  • P00/P01 operational feasibility screens;
  • public haul and fleet screening;
  • public cost/price accounting.

What is provisional:

  • the 100-year harvest level as an AAC;
  • the financial result as a budget;
  • the absence of a complete non-timber constraint set;
  • the rolling full-year haul as a continuous truck schedule;
  • the relationship between the model’s opening pattern and operational layout.

What must not be claimed:

  • that low-44 is an approved AAC;
  • that the NPV is an audited financial forecast;
  • that the current scenarios carry every MKRF ecological constraint;
  • that the rolling haul aggregate is a continuous 365-day schedule.

8. What field and data work would buy

For MKRF, the highest-value next work is not more dashboard. It is:

  1. confirm the current management zones and non-timber constraints with MKRF staff;
  2. review the opening-size and patch metrics against operational layout;
  3. validate the cost and price bases against the public accounting boundary;
  4. test a continuous multi-day haul schedule rather than monthly windows;
  5. complete a sensitivity analysis on the target weights and growing-stock floor.

The existing data are sufficient to review scenario direction tomorrow. They are not sufficient to sign a final plan.

9. Staged workplan

Before the review

  • Circulate this brief and the three-instance portal.
  • Confirm that low-44 is the scenario to open first.
  • Confirm the P00/P01 operational handoff is the right first-decade story.

After the review

  • Add MKRF staff review of zones and constraints.
  • Build the missing ecological constraint case.
  • Refresh the financial and operational evidence before any external release.

Decision gate

Do not move from “draft scenario screen” to “approved management plan” until the non-timber constraints, AAC interpretation, and financial boundary are reviewed.

10. What we are asking for

We are asking UBC Research Forests leadership and the Dean’s Office to:

  1. endorse this as the draft MKRF management-plan structure;
  2. support a focused review of zones, constraints and the low/base/high scenario family;
  3. treat the current results as a public planning screen, not an approved AAC or budget.
