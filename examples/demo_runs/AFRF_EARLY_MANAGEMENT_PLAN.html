AFRF — Gavin Lake and Knife Creek — Early Draft Management Plan and Modelling Brief

Status: Draft for discussion. Not an approved management plan, not a legal compliance determination, and not an audited financial forecast.

Prepared for: AFRF leadership and the Dean’s Office

Purpose: Show the structure of the management plan, the modelling approach, the scenario plan, and early preview results now, so the faculty can decide whether to support a focused calibration and review programme before committing to a full field campaign.

1. Executive summary

We have a working, reproducible modelling stack for the AFRF estates:

  • a full-estate Gavin Lake policy screen;
  • a full-block Knife Creek screening chain;
  • a LiDAR-informed Knife Creek spatial-clump screen;
  • a separate MKRF full-estate scenario set that exercises the same stack at a much larger scale.

The Knife Creek work now has a coherent draft management story:

  • 3,481.77 ha of VRI blocks inside the Knife Creek SUP;
  • 160 spatial blocks;
  • HMZ-restricted silviculture;
  • a four-to-eight-entry FVS-BC state-transition library;
  • a compiled WS4 dataset with HMZ-specific track offers;
  • a 300-year sustained legal-HMZ policy screen;
  • a 2024 LiDAR canopy inventory with 278,318 canopy trees, spatial clumps, and a first block-level FVS clump screen.

The early numbers are useful for structure and direction:

The Knife numbers are now plausible enough to support an early draft, but they are not yet defensible as a final AAC or budget. The main missing ingredient is not more model plumbing. It is field calibration: measured DBH, species composition, mortality, damage, and a CFI or released tree list for Knife Creek.

2. Why this matters now

The Dean’s Office needs to see enough structure and evidence to judge whether this is a credible path, without waiting for a full field campaign.

This brief is deliberately written to separate:

  1. what is already strong enough to support a management-plan structure;
  2. what is provisional and should be labelled as such; and
  3. what cannot be claimed without field calibration or legal review.

That separation is the difference between a useful early draft and a result that looks more certain than it is.

3. Management context and draft objectives

3.1 Resource

  • Knife Creek SUP S15382: 3,533.13 ha legal gross boundary.
  • Modelled VRI block area: 3,481.77 ha.
  • 160 spatial blocks after the VRI/HMZ join.
  • BEC: predominantly IDFdk3, with IDFxm at the margins.
  • Mule-deer HMZ is a first-order constraint:
    • HMZ 1/3/4 (M): Low or Moderate only;
    • HMZ 2 (L): Low only;
    • HMZ 5 (A): all classes, effectively absent inside the SUP.
  • The current work uses the public HMZ layer and the legal matrix as a structural constraint. It is not a legal compliance opinion.

3.2 Draft management objectives

  1. Maintain continuous forest cover and the uneven-aged stand structure required by the Knife Creek GWM context.
  2. Respect the HMZ offer matrix and retain the 4–10-tree clump and canopy-opening structure.
  3. Produce a sustainable, defensible harvest level without treating the first model result as an approved AAC.
  4. Maintain or improve mule-deer winter-range habitat indicators.
  5. Provide a transparent, reproducible evidence trail from inventory to treatment to schedule to finance.
  6. Build a plan that can be recalibrated when CFI and LiDAR calibration data arrive, rather than rebuilding the framework.

3.3 Draft management plan structure

The early draft should follow this structure:

  1. Purpose, legal context and decision framework
  2. Resource description and information quality
  3. Management objectives, constraints and zones
  4. Silvicultural system and GWM/HMZ rules
  5. Growth, yield and state-transition model
  6. Harvest scheduling, AAC and scenario logic
  7. Access, hauling, landing and operational feasibility
  8. Habitat, biodiversity and mule-deer monitoring
  9. Financial analysis and sensitivity
  10. Adaptive management, review triggers and governance
  11. Appendices: data provenance, model cards, run register, assumptions

This is the structure we recommend bringing to the Dean’s Office. The current brief fills in the modelling and early-results sections; the legal and silvicultural sections still need internal review.

3.4 Gavin Lake mule-deer constraint review

The 2007 AFRF Management and Working Plan #3 is explicit that mule-deer winter range is a first-order constraint on the Gavin Lake block, not a Knife-only issue.

What the plan says

  • Gavin Lake block: about 6,315 ha in the plan narrative; 6,355 ha gross in Table 26.
  • Beaver Valley Mule Deer Winter Range: 2,846 ha gross, 29% of the Gavin Lake block, managed by group selection with a 40-year re-entry period.
  • The MP#3 target is 45% of the Gavin block in mule-deer winter range, compared with the CCLUP minimum of 18%.
  • Beaver Valley MDWR is in the transition snowpack zone, not the shallow / moderate IDF zone used at Knife Creek.
  • Table 30 sets the target age structure by habitat class:
Habitat class Area harvested per pass Cutting cycle Age cohorts Maximum oldest-cohort age
High 33% 40 years 3 120 years
Moderate 25% 40 years 4 160 years
Low 20% 40 years 5 200 years
  • Maximum group size is 0.4 ha on warm aspects and 0.7 ha on other aspects, with flexibility for frost, windthrow, or site limitations.
  • Regeneration should favour Douglas-fir, with spruce, lodgepole pine, birch, aspen, subalpine fir, and cottonwood in the species preference list.
  • Preparatory thinning should generally precede group selection by at least ten years; windthrow must be monitored and salvaged promptly.

What the current Gavin scenarios do

gavin-policy is a timber-only screen. It comes from the legacy H matrix, which has one treatment: clearcut at age 80 or older. It does not carry the WORKING_CI attribute or the Beaver Valley MDWR objective.

Joining gavin-policy harvest decisions back to the legacy spatial fragments shows the scale of the gap:

  • 2,799 ha of the legacy spatial layer are mapped as MDWR.
  • gavin-policy schedules 2,759 ha of that MDWR area — 98.6%.
  • All 8,980 scheduled events are CC; 4,352 of them fall in MDWR.
  • The Gavin block layer shown in the console does not expose WORKING_CI, habitat class, or MDWR status.

So the current Gavin result is useful as a timber-only upper bound. A second screen, gavin-mdwr, now carries the no-clearcut MDWR constraint and a Table 30 group-selection approximation. It schedules 0 MDWR clearcuts, 5.626M m³ over 300 years, and meets 15/33/13 of the existing policy targets. That is a trade-off screen, not a decision-grade result.

The habitat-class assignment in gavin-mdwr now uses the official BC long-term habitat objective fine-map polygons. The adjusted Appendix 9 compartment map and legal order still need review. The screen also uses a full-area partial-cut volume approximation rather than spatial groups, and the corrected 40-year re-entry preserves stand age instead of resetting it.

Opportunities to strengthen the Gavin scenarios

  1. Reconcile the legal habitat map. The gavin-mdwr screen now uses the official BC long-term objective fine-map classes. The adjusted Appendix 9 compartment map, 2007 legal order and current UWR boundary still need review before this is a compliance claim.
  2. Add the spatial group layout. Enforce the 0.4 ha warm-aspect and 0.7 ha other-aspect group-size caps, and represent the 3–5 cohort structure.
  3. Add the preparatory thinning pass. The 2007 plan expects thinning at least ten years before group selection.
  4. Add habitat accounts. Report MDWR area scheduled, habitat-class area, residual basal area, canopy cover, cohort age distribution, maximum group size, and the proportion of the working circle in each habitat class.
  5. Run the remaining Gavin scenarios:
    • gavin-mdwr-floor: MDWR no-clearcut, with deferral rather than group selection (the current screen reports 3.560M m³ / 300 years);
    • gavin-mdwr-mp3: 45% MDWR target with the verified habitat-class map.
  6. Add a CCLUP-minimum sensitivity. Test 18% MDWR as the lower policy envelope against the MP#3 45% target, so the trade-off is explicit.
  7. Protect the Gavin Lake Demonstration Area separately. The 2007 plan gives recreation and visual quality primacy over timber in the 706 ha Demo Area; the current policy also clearcuts most of it. This is not a mule-deer constraint, but it belongs in the same scenario review.
  8. Verify the current legal order. The 2007 MWP covers 2007–2012; the current approved order, UWR boundary, and habitat-class map must be checked before any compliance claim.

The point is not to make Gavin look like Knife Creek. The point is to make the Gavin screen carry the constraint that the 2007 plan actually applies to Gavin Lake.

4. Modelling approach

4.1 Evidence chain

The stack is deliberately traceable:

public inventory / LiDAR / PSP
        ↓
FVS-BC state-transition library
        ↓
canonical state graph + HMZ tracks
        ↓
WS4 compiled spatial dataset
        ↓
FHOPS / WS4 schedule and scenario simulation
        ↓
operational screens, finance envelope, run register

The important design choice is that the state-transition library is the bridge. Every track is tied to an FVS-BC state, a selection regime, a stem list, and a run record. A track with no provenance is treated as a curve, not evidence.

4.2 FVS-BC state library

Current Knife Creek library:

  • eight 30-year entries per stand/regime;
  • 216 per-chain states;
  • 192 transitions;
  • 35 canonical states;
  • 48 canonical transitions;
  • two canonical cycles;
  • clump-harvest volume counted in the transition cut.

The clump-volume correction is material. Earlier versions counted only the FVS selection cut and left post-FVS clump harvest out of the reported yield. That made a productive cycle look like a zero-yield reserve cycle.

4.3 Spatial dataset and HMZ

ws4/dataset_kc_spatial_v5:

  • 160 blocks;
  • 140 tracks: 35 canonical states × 4 HMZ groups;
  • 96 treatments;
  • HMZ offers enforced through track copies;
  • ws4 validate passes.

The dataset is a structural fixture, not a KC operating model. Its tree lists are PSP-derived analogs; its clump reserve is a proxy unless the LiDAR clump path is used.

4.4 LiDAR

The 2024 LidarBC inventory provides:

  • 278,318 canopy trees;
  • 160/160 blocks with coverage;
  • 117,297 trees at least 20 m tall;
  • 6,046 four-to-ten-tree clumps at 10 m linkage;
  • 88,640 reserve trees and 28,191 harvest trees in the spatial reserve scenario.

The current spatial-clump FVS pilot:

  • takes a one-hectare LiDAR sample per block;
  • groups trees at least 20 m into 10 m clumps;
  • splits clumps into 4–10-tree groups;
  • reserves the three largest trees per clump;
  • harvests the remaining clump trees;
  • treats singletons as reserved;
  • uses a stable three-digit FVS plot key so after-treatment rows can be traced back to the source clump;
  • scales LiDAR TPH to VRI live stems per hectare;
  • scales LiDAR DBH so block BA matches VRI BA;
  • samples tree-level species from the VRI species composition.

This is a real spatial-clump implementation, but it is still a structural pilot. The LiDAR DBH is height-allometry, the species are sampled from VRI composition, and the one-hectare sample is scaled to VRI TPH/BA.

4.5 Operational and financial layer

FHOPS and WS4 operational screens provide:

  • block-level harvest decisions;
  • landing and machine constraints;
  • haul distance and truck-fleet screens;
  • period production and target reports;
  • public finance envelopes.

The finance values are an AFRF MWP #3 Figure 7 public envelope. They are not a budget forecast. NPV uses annual cash flows spread uniformly inside 10-year periods.

5.3 Scenario logic

The plan should carry three Knife Creek cases forward:

  1. Legal-HMZ GWM base case — the constraint-respecting screen.
  2. LiDAR-informed spatial clump case — the case that tests whether observed clump geometry changes the harvest level.
  3. Stress/sensitivity case — low/base/high finance, plus a no-harvest or reserve-heavy habitat case.

The decision rule should not be “pick the highest NPV.” It should be:

  • does the case meet the HMZ constraints?
  • is the growth/yield evidence strong enough for the claimed harvest level?
  • does the operational screen close?
  • does the habitat indicator stay within the intended range?
  • can we explain the result without hiding the uncertainty?

8. What field data buys

A focused calibration programme would change the quality of the plan in four specific ways:

  1. Measured DBH and BA. Calibrates the LiDAR height–DBH relation and replaces the VRI scaling assumption.
  2. Species composition. Replaces VRI-sampled species with measured tree-level composition.
  3. Mortality, damage and ingrowth. Makes the state transitions defensible instead of structural.
  4. CFI or released tree list. Anchors the growth and yield model to Knife Creek rather than a regional PSP analog.

The field programme does not need to cover the whole estate at once. A stratified pilot across the main HMZ/BEC/productivity strata would be enough to test whether the current results are credible. If the pilot shows the model is tracking, it justifies a larger campaign. If it does not, we learn that early, before the plan is presented as final.

9. Staged workplan

Next 2–4 weeks

  • Internal review of this draft and the scenario logic.
  • Choose calibration strata and sample design.
  • Validate the LiDAR clump layer against VRI and available PSP plots.
  • Fix or explicitly document the small number of blocks that fail the current FVS row mapping.
  • Produce a legal/HMZ review checklist.
  • Add Gavin WORKING_CI and the MDWR no-clearcut constraint; build the first Gavin MDWR scenario.

Next field season

  • CFI/PSP remeasurement in the selected strata.
  • Measured species, DBH, height, damage and mortality.
  • LiDAR plot calibration and, if possible, point-cloud CHM improvement.
  • Re-run the FVS-BC library with calibrated tree lists.

Next 4–6 months

  • Rebuild the state library and WS4 dataset.
  • Re-run legal-HMZ, LiDAR-gated and spatial-clump scenarios.
  • Complete the operational and finance review.
  • Produce the first full draft management plan for internal review.

Decision gate

Do not treat this as an approved AAC or budget. The gate is:

  • calibrated growth/yield evidence;
  • HMZ/legal review;
  • operational feasibility;
  • habitat indicators;
  • transparent finance envelope.

10. What we are asking for

We are asking the Dean’s Office to support:

  1. Endorsement of the draft structure and scenario framework as the basis for consultation, not as an approved plan. This includes correcting the Gavin Lake description from “decision-grade” to “timber-only upper bound until the Beaver Valley MDWR constraint is carried.”
  2. A focused calibration and technical-review programme — field pilot, LiDAR calibration, legal/HMZ review, and model review.
  3. A staged resource decision — small calibration first, larger field campaign only if the early results remain credible.

The current work is strong enough to show the structure, the modelling approach, the scenario plan and preliminary results. It is not strong enough to defend a final AAC, a final revenue forecast, or a legal compliance claim. That is the honest boundary, and it is the right basis for a Dean’s Office conversation.
