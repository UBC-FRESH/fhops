fhops.hauling Package

Pydantic contracts for operational log hauling.

The hauling layer is an optional extension to fhops.scenario.contract.Scenario. It adds the entities that the harvesting and tactical-operational models do not carry: log sorts, contractors, truck types and trucks, depots, sort yards, haul arcs, loader location bindings, and the driver policy from Ghotb (2023).

The contract is deliberately a data layer. It validates identifiers, units, ranges, and cross references; it does not build or solve an optimisation model. The Phase I flow model, Phase II scheduling model, and Phase III simulated-annealing solver live in sibling modules.

References

Ghotb, S. (2023). Optimization of Log Logistics at the Operational Level Considering Sorting Decisions and Synchronization Requirements. PhD thesis, University of British Columbia. <https://hdl.handle.net/2429/85563>

Phase I monthly flow model for operational log hauling.

This module implements the first phase of Ghotb’s decomposition: a bi-objective mixed-integer program for daily sorted and unsorted log flows from blocks to sort yards, with yard processing, inventory, contractor compatibility, fleet capacity, and contractor workload balance. The bi-objective model is solved by goal programming.

The formulation is documented in notes/hauling_formulation_audit.md (equations 3-1 to 3-23). The implementation deliberately tightens the thesis in three places:

  • compatibility is enforced by index restriction instead of a big-M;

  • the absolute workload deviation is linearised with two inequalities;

  • goal deviations are non-negative continuous variables (the thesis/paper disagree on which one is integer).

The model is built from a validated fhops.scenario.contract.Scenario with a hauling: section. It does not import or modify the operational or tactical MILPs.

Phase II continuous-time daily truck routing and scheduling.

This module implements the second phase of Ghotb’s decomposition: a mixed-integer program that assigns Phase I truckloads to compatible trucks and contractors, routes the trucks from a central depot, and synchronises trucks with stationary loaders at pickup and delivery. Time is continuous, so the model returns arrival, waiting, service, and return times rather than slot assignments.

The formulation follows Chapter 4 of Ghotb (2023), with the corrections recorded in notes/hauling_formulation_audit.md:

  • each truckload is assigned to exactly one compatible truck;

  • each truck has a start/end route and a sequence of loads, with MTZ subtour elimination;

  • each pickup and delivery load is assigned to one loader at its location;

  • loaders have a total order and no overlapping service;

  • trucks and loaders share the service start time, so waiting is the gap between arrival and start;

  • regular time and overtime are separate variables, so overtime is charged exactly.

The exact MIP is intended for small and medium daily instances. The simulated-annealing solver in P11.4 handles the large case.

Marine barge-cycle scheduling and port synchronization.

The road scheduler in fhops.hauling.schedule routes trucks and loaders. A marine route adds a second equipment class: a barge that loads at an origin port, sails a water arc, unloads at a destination port, and returns empty. This module turns the Phase I barge trips into a deterministic single-day barge schedule with:

  • one row per barge trip;

  • port-loader assignment and non-overlapping terminal service;

  • barge load, sail, unload, and empty-return times;

  • the synchronized road pickup and delivery windows for the drayage legs.

The deterministic scheduler is deliberately small and auditable. It complements the exact truck MIP and the SA solver; it does not replace either. The exact truck MIP schedules the road loads (including the drayage legs when a marine route is present), while this module schedules the water leg. The returned road windows are the handoff between the two schedules and are reported, not hard-linked, in the first release.

Phase III simulated annealing for daily log-truck scheduling.

This module implements the solution approach of Ghotb (2023), Chapter 5: a permutation-based representation of truck routes, a shortest-processing-time (SPT) schedule decoder, swap/reversion/ insertion neighbourhoods, and a geometric cooling schedule. It is the large-instance path; the exact MIP in fhops.hauling.schedule remains the oracle for small days.

The decoder is deliberately built on top of fhops.hauling.schedule.simulate_plan(), so the timing logic is shared with the exact-model verification oracle. It derives loader orders from projected arrival times (SPT) and then recomputes the earliest schedule for the fixed routes and loader orders.
